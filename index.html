Bittle-servo crane

A two-joint arm with a gripper, driven by three Petoi Bittle servos. Both arm servos sit on the base; a parallelogram linkage carries the elbow drive up the arm.

Try it

Blue: driven links. Orange: the passive parallelogram (crank on the base, rod parallel to the upper arm). Grey: the optional levelling parallelogram. Angles from horizontal. Torques are static, at the current pose, with ideal frictionless joints.

The idea

Stacking servos puts each motor's 14 g on the joint below it, so the shoulder lifts the elbow servo as dead load. Here the elbow servo S2 sits on the base, coaxial with the shoulder S1. It turns a short crank. A rod, the same length as the upper arm and parallel to it, joins the crank tip to a lever on the back of the forearm. Crank, rod, forearm lever and upper arm form a parallelogram, a four-bar whose coupler stays parallel to the frame (the coupler and four-bar lab in the handling lab, after Berger 2021, ch. 4).

Two things follow:

  • Decoupled joints. The forearm angle measured from the ground equals the crank angle, whatever the shoulder does. Moving S1 lifts the forearm without tilting it, which makes crane work (keep the load level, lift it) a single-servo move.
  • Light arm. Only the gripper servo rides on the arm. A second parallelogram, from a fixed bar on the base through the elbow to the wrist, keeps the gripper level with no motor at all.

The price: the parallelogram jams when crank and upper arm line up (dead centre). The relative angle between upper arm and forearm must stay inside about 30° to 150°; the demo turns red outside it and the firmware should clamp it.

Servo

Petoi lists the P1S as a slim, fast servo with 270° of controllable travel. A RobotShop listing of the Bittle 10-servo set gives 3 kg·cm stall torque, 14 g, 30 × 24 × 12 mm, 0.07 s/60° and 6.0–8.4 V. That listing does not name the P1S, so treat 3 kg·cm as unconfirmed and measure it before relying on the margins below.

Control

Because the joints are decoupled, the servo angles come straight from the pose, with no cross term:

S1 = θ_upper_arm           // from horizontal
S2 = θ_forearm             // absolute, through the parallelogram
check 30° ≤ θ_upper − θ_forearm ≤ 150°   // keep clear of dead centre

Inverse kinematics for a wrist target (x, y) with equal links L: d = √(x²+y²), α = atan2(y, x), β = acos(d / 2L), then θ_upper = α + β, θ_forearm = α − β.

Limits

  • A planar arm: no base rotation, as asked (two degrees of freedom plus the gripper). A turntable servo can be added under the base later without changing the arm.
  • Static torques only; servo speed, friction and joint play are left out.
  • Masses are estimates; the stall torque is from a reseller listing, not a Petoi datasheet.
  • Not built or tested yet.
